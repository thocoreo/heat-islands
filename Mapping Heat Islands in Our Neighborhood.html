The question

Do surfaces and spaces on the same block differ in temperature, and do surface material and tree cover explain the difference?

Hypothesis: asphalt, concrete, and rooftops will run hotter than grass or shaded ground, with the biggest gap in the early afternoon.

How we measured

  • 6 sites: asphalt road, concrete sidewalk, rooftop, bare soil, grass, and tree shade.
  • 4 times a day: 8 am, noon, 2 pm, and 6 pm, for 5 days.
  • Two readings per stop: surface temperature with an infrared thermometer, and air temperature 1 m above ground in a white shade shield.
  • Weather log: clear skies on all days, light wind.

The six sites, colored by their 2 pm surface temperature.

    What it shows

    25 °C surface gap

    At 2 pm, asphalt (57 °C) was 25 °C hotter than shaded ground (32 °C).

    Only 3.8 °C in the air

    Air temperature differed far less, from 33.0 °C under the tree to 36.8 °C over asphalt.

    Peak at 2 pm

    Every site peaked at 2 pm. Hard surfaces were still 12 to 15 °C above shade at 6 pm.

    Conclusions

    • The hypothesis held: dark, hard surfaces got hottest, and tree shade stayed coolest all day.
    • Surface temperature is a much bigger signal than air temperature, but the air still warmed by nearly 4 °C near asphalt.
    • Grass was about 20 °C cooler than asphalt at 2 pm, so even small green patches help.
    • Practical takeaway: shade trees, reflective roofs, and lighter paving are the most direct ways to cool a block.

    Limits

    Five days and six sites are a small sample. Infrared readings depend on surface texture and color. A real study should add more days, cloudy conditions, and a comparison with a weather station.
